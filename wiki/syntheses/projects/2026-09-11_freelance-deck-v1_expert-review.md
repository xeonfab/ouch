## Method

For each card: (1) is the administrative mechanism right (organisme, procedure, order of events)? (2) is the figure plausible and non-regulatory (regulatory thresholds change yearly and never go in a card)? (3) is the entity the right one, and should one be tagged at all? (4) Type A (a third party can build around it) or B (only the named organisation can fix it)? (5) frequency, honest: **large** (most freelances meet it), **medium** (a clear segment), **niche** (a specific status or situation). No personalised advice; regulatory facts are to be checked against the official source at insertion time and dated in the sourcing sheet.

## Corrections applied to the deck

| Card | Problem found | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| F03 | « 60 jours fin de mois » exceeds the legal maximum for B2B payment terms (60 days net, or 45 days end of month). A card that states an unlawful term as a norm invites the wrong debate | « paie à 60 jours : livré début janvier, payé fin mars » ; title « deux mois de trésorerie » |
| F08 | An injonction de payer is cheap in money (court fee, no lawyer needed); the real cost is time and paperwork | Statement and title now say *time*, not money |
| F16 | A client's terms imposed on a supplier are conditions générales d'**achat**, not CGV (which are the freelance's own terms) | « conditions d'achat » ; hashtag `#ConditionsDAchat` |
| F35 | The obligation above the CA threshold is a **dedicated** account, not a "pro" account; the friction is that banks often refuse to let a personal account serve and push a paid pro account | Statement now says the bank only accepted a pro account; title « devient un compte pro payant » |
| F22 | The attestation is requested per contract above a threshold, every six months | Title « par client et par contrat » |
